Your SSA statement assumes you keep working until you claim. This tool shows you what your benefits actually look like if you stop working early — and delay claiming.

⚠ What Your SSA Statement Doesn't Show You

Every benefit estimate on your Social Security statement assumes you continue earning your current salary right up until the day you claim. If you retire at 55 and wait until 70 to claim, that $3,949 number is wrong — it assumes 15 years of earnings that will never happen. This prompt calculates what you'll actually get.

The Prompt

Copy this — then paste it into your AI with your SSA statement

early-retirement-ss-calculator.txt
I need help calculating what my Social Security benefits would be if I stop working early, rather than continuing to work as SSA assumes. 🚨 CRITICAL CONTEXT: My Social Security statement shows benefits at ages 62, 67, and 70 — but these numbers assume I continue working until the age I CLAIM benefits, not just until I retire. For example, if my statement shows $3,949 at age 70, that assumes I work continuously until age 70 and then claim. If I actually retire at 55 and wait to claim until 70, that $3,949 is completely wrong because it assumes 15 years of earnings that will never happen. I'm uploading my Social Security statement PDF. Please: 1. Extract my complete earnings history from the statement 2. Identify my 35 highest INDEXED earning years (see indexing instructions below) 3. Calculate my current AIME and PIA 4. Show me what happens to my benefits if I STOP WORKING at different ages but WAIT TO CLAIM later Calculate scenarios for: - Stop working NOW (my current age) - Stop working at age 55 - Stop working at age 56 - Stop working at age 57 - Stop working at age 58 - Stop working at age 60 - Stop working at age 62 For each stop-work age, show me: - Monthly SS benefit if I claim at age 62 - Monthly SS benefit if I claim at FRA (age 66 or 67 depending on birth year) - Monthly SS benefit if I claim at age 70 - The percentage reduction from SSA's projections Present results in a clear table. Make it explicit that "Stop at 55, Claim at 70" is VERY different from SSA's assumption of "Work until 70, Claim at 70." ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━ 🚨 CRITICAL CALCULATION STEP — WAGE INDEXING (DO NOT SKIP) Before calculating AIME, every year's earnings MUST be indexed to the year the person turns 60. Raw nominal earnings CANNOT be used directly — this is the most common calculation error AI models make with Social Security. The indexing formula is: Indexed Earnings = Nominal Earnings × (AWI in age-60 year ÷ AWI in earnings year) Use the following Average Wage Index (AWI) values: 1985: $16,823 1986: $17,321 1987: $18,426 1988: $19,334 1989: $20,099 1990: $21,028 1991: $21,812 1992: $22,935 1993: $23,133 1994: $23,754 1995: $24,706 1996: $25,914 1997: $27,426 1998: $28,861 1999: $30,470 2000: $32,155 2001: $32,922 2002: $33,252 2003: $34,064 2004: $35,648 2005: $36,952 2006: $38,651 2007: $40,405 2008: $41,335 2009: $40,712 2010: $41,674 2011: $42,979 2012: $44,322 2013: $44,888 2014: $46,481 2015: $48,099 2016: $48,642 2017: $50,321 2018: $52,146 2019: $54,099 2020: $55,628 2021: $60,575 2022: $63,795 2023: $66,621 2024: $68,869 (est.) For years after the known AWI values, project forward at 3% annual growth. For earnings AT or AFTER the person's age-60 year, use nominal value (index factor = 1.0). ✅ VERIFICATION CHECK — run this before proceeding: A person who earned $3,500 in 1986 and turns 60 in 2030 should have an indexed value of approximately $16,500–$17,000 for that year — NOT $3,500. If your AIME calculation produces a number close to simply dividing total NOMINAL earnings by 420, you have skipped indexing and your results are wrong. Stop and redo the calculation with proper indexing before continuing. ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━ BEND POINTS — use 2024 values: • 90% of first $1,174 of AIME • 32% of AIME between $1,174 and $7,078 • 15% of AIME above $7,078 CLAIMING AGE ADJUSTMENTS: • Age 62: 70% of PIA • FRA (67 for born 1960+): 100% of PIA • Age 70: 124% of PIA IMPORTANT — HOW PIA AND CLAIMING AGE INTERACT: • PIA is determined by when you STOP WORKING (which sets your AIME) • Your actual benefit = PIA × claiming age adjustment • These are two separate steps — SSA statements conflate them • "Stop work at 55, claim at 70" = (PIA based on 55 stop-work) × 1.24 • This is VERY different from SSA's "work to 70, claim at 70" figure Show your work: display the AIME and PIA for each stop-work scenario before applying the claiming age adjustments, so I can verify the calculations. Note at the end: AWI projections beyond 2024 are estimated and actual SSA calculations will use official published AWI values. Treat all results as planning estimates, not guaranteed benefit amounts.
Results Guide

What good results look like

✓ Signs the AI calculated correctly
  • Shows indexed earnings — early years significantly higher than nominal
  • Displays AIME and PIA separately for each scenario
  • AIME is roughly 5x–7x higher than simple nominal average for most workers
  • Reductions vs SSA statement are in the 8%–25% range for typical stop-work ages
  • Shows verification step explicitly
✗ Signs the AI skipped indexing
  • Early earnings (1980s–1990s) look the same as nominal amounts
  • AIME appears close to total nominal earnings ÷ 420
  • Reductions vs SSA statement are 40%–55% — far too large
  • FRA benefit for stop-at-55 scenario is under $2,000 for average earners
  • No mention of indexing or AWI in the calculation steps
Important: These calculations are estimates for planning purposes only. Actual Social Security benefits are determined by the SSA using official Average Wage Index values published annually. Results should be used to understand the general impact of early retirement on benefits — not as a guarantee of future payments. Always verify important decisions with the SSA directly at ssa.gov/myaccount or with a qualified financial advisor.
