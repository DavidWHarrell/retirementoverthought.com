Early Retirement SS Calculator

What happens to your Social Security if you stop working early?

The Problem

Your Social Security statement assumes you'll keep working at your current income until you claim benefits. But what if you retire at 55 instead of 65? Your benefit will be lower because those missing work years get replaced with zeros in the calculation.

This tool shows you exactly how much your SS benefit changes if you stop working early.

Step 3: Copy This Prompt

I need help calculating what my Social Security benefits would be if I stop working early, rather than continuing to work as SSA assumes. I'm uploading my Social Security statement PDF. Please: 1. Extract my complete earnings history from the statement 2. Identify my 35 highest indexed earning years 3. Calculate my current AIME (Average Indexed Monthly Earnings) and PIA (Primary Insurance Amount) 4. Show me what happens to my benefits if I stop working at different ages: - If I stop working NOW (my current age) - If I stop at age 55 - If I stop at age 60 - If I stop at age 62 For each stop-work scenario, show me: - What my monthly SS benefit would be at age 62 - What my monthly SS benefit would be at age 67 (FRA) - What my monthly SS benefit would be at age 70 - How much this differs from SSA's projections (which assume continued work) Please present the results in a clear table format so I can compare scenarios. Use 2024 bend points for calculations: - 90% of first $1,174 of AIME - 32% of AIME between $1,174 and $7,078 - 15% of AIME above $7,078 Account for early/delayed claiming adjustments: - Age 62: 70% of PIA - Age 67: 100% of PIA (Full Retirement Age) - Age 70: 124% of PIA
